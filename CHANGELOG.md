# Report Changelog

This log tracks updates, methodology iterations, and corrections for the Statistics Canada AI Visibility Study report.

## Jul 27, 2026

- **Asked three times each — the box is steady, the numbers aren't.** Every Wave 1 question was re-asked on the ordinary Bing results page, three separate times, for 33 captures in total. Whether an AI answer box appeared at all was perfectly consistent — not a single question changed its behaviour across the three runs. But the figures inside those answers did move: asked about loneliness in Canada, the same question on the same day returned 13% twice and 40% once. Separately, the old count of "how often there was no AI answer" was re-checked properly, and 3 of the 10 questions filed that way turn out to get full answers — a 30% error rate, so that figure has been retired rather than corrected.
- **A third place to ask, and the clearest result yet.** The same questions were put to Claude, an AI assistant, in an isolated setup that knew nothing about this study. It credited Statistics Canada directly far more often than Bing did — all 11 Wave 1 questions against Bing's 4 — and never failed to answer. (Each question was put to it three times and the majority answer used; an earlier single-run count of 7 of 11 understated it.) One caution that cuts the other way: asked repeatedly, Claude was less consistent than Bing about *who* it credited — it changed its mind on 2 of the 11, where Bing never did. The striking part is *which* questions: the three Bing handles worst are the three Claude handles best. Ask whether income affects health in Canada and Bing offers a vague yes with no figures at all, while Claude gives Statistics Canada's actual numbers on all three runs — healthy life expectancy of 61.9 years in the lowest income group against 70.0 in the highest, an 8.1-year gap. The same data was sitting there for both. That points the finger not at AI ignoring official statistics, but at the search and SEO layer sitting between the public and those statistics.
- **A measurement problem, found and fixed.** When checking answers, the search page sometimes loaded results for a completely different question while the browser tab still showed the right one — a question about Canadian salaries came back with dictionary definitions of the word "average", and a question about mental health came back with results about a magazine called SELF. Those broken loads never show an AI answer, which means the earlier count of "how often there was no AI answer" was partly counting page-loading failures rather than real behaviour — so that figure has been set aside. One specific question, self-rated mental health, had been recorded as getting no answer at all; it does in fact get one, which uses a Statistics Canada survey's number but credits a different government website. The checking tool now reloads and verifies every page before recording anything.

## Jul 25, 2026

- **One place to ask, and the two waves join up.** Checked whether search engines have found the mirror pages yet — they haven't. That means the "before" measurement isn't locked in, so rather than living with two waves asked in two different places, Wave 1 is being re-asked on the ordinary Bing results page as well. From here both waves are measured in the same place, and they can be analysed as a single 17-table experiment (10 with mirrors, 7 held back) instead of two smaller ones — a real gain, since the biggest weakness of this study is how few tables it covers. This has to be finished before the pages get indexed; if they're found in a search index first, the plan reverts.
- **Surface correction.** Found that the two waves were asked on different Bing surfaces — Wave 1 in Bing's dedicated AI mode, Wave 2 on the ordinary results page — while both were labelled the same way in the data. Each wave will now be re-checked on the surface it started on (mixing them would make any before/after change a quirk of where we asked rather than a real effect), the two waves are no longer compared head-to-head, and the earlier "a quarter of the time there was no AI answer" figure is now correctly attributed to the ordinary results page rather than to Bing generally. Wave 2's internal findings are unaffected — all its subjects were asked the same way.
- **Full methodology audit** (see "How we're trying not to fool ourselves"), run before any post-treatment results exist. Named a single primary test to avoid cherry-picking; each question will now be asked several times per round because AI answers vary run to run; answers will be graded without knowing which group they belong to; and if the pages never get indexed, that will be reported as inconclusive rather than as a failed idea. Extra "before" rounds are being collected while the pages are still uncrawled, to measure how much these answers wobble on their own.
- **Scoring correction.** The Wave 2 Health scorecard now reports out of the **19 questions Statistics Canada can actually answer**, not all 25 tested. The 6 questions StatCan doesn't cover (opioid deaths, cancer projections) are set aside before scoring instead of appearing as a category — they can't test whether the AI uses StatCan's data, so including them understated the rate. No underlying codings changed; all 25 remain in the published data.
- Wave 2 treatment mirrors deployed (5 tables: mental health, family doctor, income & health, population, immigrants), each crediting Statistics Canada in its markup and citation. Immigration baseline finished on Bing. Both waves are now at the same stage — treatment live, awaiting the scheduled re-audits. Wording clarified: the "another agency" category means StatCan isn't the source for that topic, not that it was skipped.
