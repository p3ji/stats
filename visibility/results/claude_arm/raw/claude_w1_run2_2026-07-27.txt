QUESTION: 1. Are charitable donations declining in Canada?
SEARCHED: yes
ANSWER: Yes on participation, more mixed on dollars. The share of tax filers claiming charitable donations fell from 25.1% in 2002 to 21.9% in 2013 to 17.1% in 2022 and a record-low 16.8% in 2023 -- just under 5 million donors. The share of aggregate income donated also slipped, from 0.54% in 2018 to 0.50% in 2022. Total dollars donated have held up better because remaining donors (especially higher-income ones) give more, so the picture is "fewer, larger donors" rather than a collapse in total giving. Separately, the 2023 Survey on Giving, Volunteering and Participating found the donation rate fell alongside volunteering.

QUESTION: 2. What is the average salary in Canada by age?
SEARCHED: yes
ANSWER: Income rises steeply through the 20s and 30s, peaks in the 45-54 band, and falls after 55 as people move to part-time work and retirement. Using Statistics Canada's income-of-individuals table, median total income runs roughly $48,000 for ages 25-34, about $60,000 for 35-44, about $60,000-$62,000 for 45-54 (the peak), somewhat lower for 55-64, and around $35,000-$40,000 for 65+ where pensions and OAS/CPP dominate. Under-25s are well below all of these, typically in the $15,000-$25,000 range because of part-time and student work. I'd flag two caveats: these are *total income* (all sources, all individuals with income), not salaries of full-time workers, which run considerably higher; and the exact year matters, so the live table is the thing to check.

QUESTION: 3. What is the average salary in Canada by province?
SEARCHED: yes

By province: Ontario $1,357.26, Alberta $1,353.54, British Columbia $1,310.62, Newfoundland and Labrador $1,293.43, Saskatchewan $1,277.40, Quebec $1,259.63, New Brunswick $1,193.47, Nova Scotia $1,179.42, Manitoba $1,177.18, and Prince Edward Island lowest at $1,141.86. QUESTION: 4. Volunteering in Canada -- what do the statistics say?
SEARCHED: yes
ANSWER: The headline from the 2023 Survey on Giving, Volunteering and Participating is a broad decline. Formal volunteering (through a nonprofit or charity) fell from 44% in 2013 to 41% in 2018 to 32% in 2023, and informal volunteering (helping people directly, outside any organization) fell from 74% to 66% over 2018-2023. Combining both, about 73% of Canadians volunteered in some form in 2023, averaging 173 hours each -- down 33 hours from 2018. Total formal volunteer hours dropped to roughly 1.2 billion, down more than a quarter from about 1.7 billion in 2018. The load is also concentrated: a small minority of volunteers contributes the large majority of hours.

QUESTION: 5. What percentage of Canadians volunteer?
SEARCHED: yes
ANSWER: It depends which definition you use. For formal volunteering through a charity or nonprofit, 32% of Canadians aged 15+ volunteered in 2023 -- down from 41% in 2018 and 44% in 2013. For informal volunteering (directly helping neighbours, friends, or others outside an organization), it was 66% in 2023, down from 74% in 2018. Counting either type, about 73% volunteered in 2023. The number most people mean by "volunteer rate" is the formal one: roughly one in three.

QUESTION: 6. Who does more housework, men or women, in Canada?
SEARCHED: yes
ANSWER: Women, consistently and by a wide margin. Statistics Canada's satellite account estimated women did about 820 hours of unpaid housework in 2019 versus 540 hours for men -- roughly 50% more. In time-use terms, women aged 25-54 averaged about 3.9 hours a day on unpaid work versus 2.4 hours for men. The 2022 Time Use Survey showed the gap persists regardless of telework: among teleworkers, women spent 102.9 minutes a day on housework versus 62.5 for men; among non-teleworkers, 87.3 versus 48.5 minutes. Women also do about an hour more routine child care per day (2.3 vs 1.4 hours). The gap has narrowed over three decades -- women's chore time is down about 42 minutes a day, men's up about 24 -- and the task mix differs, with women doing more daily repetitive work (cooking, cleaning, laundry) and men more episodic work (repairs, yard work, garbage).

QUESTION: 7. How many businesses in Canada use artificial intelligence?
SEARCHED: yes
ANSWER: As of the second quarter of 2026, 19.2% of Canadian businesses reported using AI to produce goods or deliver services in the preceding 12 months. That has tripled in two years: 6.1% in Q2 2024, 12.2% in Q2 2025, 19.2% in Q2 2026. Adoption is heavily concentrated by sector -- information and cultural industries 42.3%, finance and insurance 40.4%, professional/scientific/technical services 32.4% -- while most goods-producing and small-business sectors lag well behind. Notably, as of Q3 2025 about two-thirds of businesses (66.7%) still reported no plans to adopt AI, and Statistics Canada research finds the adoption gap with the U.S. is closing faster than the associated productivity gap.

QUESTION: 8. How many Canadians work in the public sector?
SEARCHED: yes
ANSWER: About 4.5 million -- roughly 4.55 million as of December 2025, up from about 4.46 million a year earlier. That's around one in five employed Canadians. The figure covers all three levels of government plus government-funded institutions: federal, provincial and municipal administration, public schools, universities, hospitals and other health institutions, and Crown corporations. The federal public service itself is only a fraction of that, in the range of 350,000-450,000. Growth has largely flattened recently -- public sector employment was little changed over the twelve months ending April 2026 -- after a substantial run-up over 2015-2024.

QUESTION: 9. Do Canadians trust the government?
SEARCHED: yes
ANSWER: Only partly, and less than they trust most other institutions. Statistics Canada's 2023 confidence survey found just 28% of Canadians reported high confidence in the federal Parliament -- the lowest-scoring institution measured, well behind the justice system and courts (49%), the school system (45%), and police. The OECD's 2024 trust survey is somewhat kinder, putting 49% of Canadians at high or moderately high trust in the federal government in 2023 -- above the OECD average of 39% and up 4 points from 2021. So Canada looks relatively good internationally while still having a majority that does not express confidence, and trust in institutions like the media and Parliament has eroded over the past decade.

QUESTION: 10. How religious is Canada?
SEARCHED: yes
ANSWER: Affiliated on paper for most, but practising for a minority, and declining fast on both. In the 2021 Census, 34.6% of Canadians -- about 12.6 million people -- reported no religious affiliation, more than double the 16.5% of 2001. Religious affiliation overall fell from 87.5% in 1991 to 65.4% in 2021, with Christianity accounting for nearly all of the drop (about 53%, down from roughly 77% in 2001) while Islam, Hinduism and Sikhism grew, largely through immigration. Practice has fallen further than identity: monthly-or-more attendance at group religious activities dropped from 35.0% in 1991 to 23.3% in 2019, and the share saying religious or spiritual beliefs are somewhat or very important fell from 71.0% in 2003 to 53.7% in 2020. The generational split is stark -- 59.1% of under-25s reported an affiliation versus 80.9% of those 65+.

QUESTION: 11. Loneliness in Canada -- what do the statistics say?
SEARCHED: yes
ANSWER: In the first quarter of 2024, 13% of Canadians aged 15 and over said they always or often felt lonely -- more than one in ten. Contrary to the common assumption, it skews young: 17% of those aged 15-24 reported frequent loneliness, versus 14% of those 25-54, 11% of those 55-64, and 10% of those 65 and over. LGBTQ2+ Canadians reported the highest rate of any group at 27%. Loneliness also tracks strongly with other measures -- people reporting frequent loneliness report much lower life satisfaction and worse self-rated mental health -- and rates among people living alone and among those with disabilities are elevated. Statistics Canada monitors this quarterly through the Canadian Social Survey, so the series is worth checking for the latest quarter.
